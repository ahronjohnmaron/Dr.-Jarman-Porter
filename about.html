About the Author

Twenty-five years of noticing

"The most important part of my job has never happened at a desk."

Dr. Jarman Myron Porter has served at every level of education, from the classroom and the campus to district-level leadership. It happens at the door in the morning, in the cafeteria at the loudest hour of the day, in the four seconds it takes a young person to make eye contact with an adult who is actually paying attention.

In 2005, at Arkansas Baptist College, he created Campus Cleanup, a work-based alternative to punitive discipline that significantly reduced student infractions. He developed proposals on minority teacher recruitment for Kankakee School District 111 and wrote the play What A Test Can't Measure for the district's Black History Program.

His published dissertation, The Effective Collaboration Between Higher Education Institutions and Community Leaders, studies how schools and communities can genuinely build something together.

A 2012 mayoral candidate, he still repeats his line to students and superintendents alike: if you do not have a plan, you will soon have a problem.

He was a marginal student in elementary and high school — and has since earned the highest degree his field offers. He has never stopped believing the quiet student in the back of the room is holding an idea the rest of us need to hear.
